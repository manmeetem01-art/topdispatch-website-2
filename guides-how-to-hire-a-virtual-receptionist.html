How to Hire a Virtual Receptionist

A practical checklist for owners: what to prepare, what to ask providers, and how to get your new receptionist productive in the first week.

By the Top Dispatch team · Updated October 29, 2026

The short answer

To hire a virtual receptionist, first define the hours and tasks you need covered, then compare providers on whether you get a dedicated person, trade experience, software access, pricing model and contract terms. Interview the candidate, prepare your call script, price sheet and emergency rules, set up logins and call forwarding, and review call summaries daily during the first week.

1. Define what you need

List the hours that need cover and the tasks: answering, booking, dispatch, estimate follow-up, admin. Most owners start with the phones and add tasks once the receptionist knows the business.

2. Questions to ask providers

  • Will I have the same person every day?
  • Have they worked in my trade?
  • Can I interview them first?
  • Will they work inside my software?
  • How is pricing calculated, and is there a contract?
  • What happens when they're sick?

3. Prepare your playbook

Write down your greeting, services and exclusions, price sheet, service area, technician skills, on-call rota and emergency rules. This is what turns a good receptionist into your receptionist.

4. Set up access

Create a user login in your field-service software and phone system with only the permissions needed. Forward your line or add the receptionist to your ring group.

Remember to compare fully loaded costs. A local hire's wage is only the start, and one person covers one shift. A dedicated remote receptionist on a flat monthly fee often covers more hours for less, but only if they can book and dispatch, not just take messages.

Red flags when choosing a provider

  • You can't speak to the person who will answer your phone
  • Pricing is per minute with a high overage rate and no cap
  • They can't log in to your scheduling or field-service software
  • Long minimum terms or cancellation fees
  • No clear plan for sick days, holidays or turnover
  • Vague answers about who can access your customer data

5. Run the first week closely

Read call summaries daily, correct anything early and add notes to the playbook. By week two most owners are spending far less time on the phone. See how Top Dispatch onboarding works.

6. Score it after 30 days

Judge the hire on results, not impressions. After a month, look at: share of calls answered live, jobs booked from inbound calls, callbacks from customers asking where the tech is, open estimates followed up, and how much of your own day you got back. If the numbers are not moving, the script or the fit needs work, and a good provider will help fix either.
