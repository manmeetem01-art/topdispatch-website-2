What does a remote dispatcher do for a home service company?

A remote dispatcher manages your schedule board from offsite. They assign new jobs to the right technician by skill and location, call or message techs with job details, reshuffle the day when a job runs long, send customers arrival-time updates and route emergencies to your on-call technician, all inside your existing field-service software.

Why dispatch is where small shops lose the most money

Answering the phone gets the job. Dispatch decides whether you make money on it. A poorly routed day means techs driving across town twice, a high-value install waiting behind a filter change, and customers calling to ask where the truck is.

In most small home service businesses, dispatch is done by the owner from the cab of a truck, between jobs, with a phone in one hand. It works until the business has three or four techs. Then schedule clashes, missed close-outs and late arrivals start costing real money and reviews.

A dedicated remote dispatcher keeps the board under control all day. They know which tech is certified for which work, who is closest, which jobs are running long and which customers need a heads-up. You get the benefit of an in-house dispatcher without adding someone to the office payroll.

What your dispatcher does through the day

Dispatch is a running loop, not a single task. Your dispatcher works it continuously:

What we need from you

  • Your technicians, skills, certifications and start/finish times
  • Territories or zones each tech usually covers
  • Priority rules: what jumps the queue and what can wait
  • Your on-call rota and escalation chain
  • Close-out requirements: photos, notes, signatures, parts
  • Dispatcher access to your field-service software and team messaging
Step 1

Completed, carried-over and follow-up jobs listed for you and tomorrow's board pre-checked.

Dispatch Coordination FAQ

Straight answers to the questions owners ask most.

Can a remote dispatcher really manage my technicians?

Yes. Dispatching happens in your software and over phone and text, which works the same from any location. Your dispatcher calls and messages techs exactly as an in-office dispatcher would.

Which field-service software do you work with?

Common ones include ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, Service Fusion and Workiz. If you run something else, your dispatcher learns it during onboarding.

How do emergency calls get dispatched?

You set the emergency rules: what counts as urgent, who is on call and when to wake you. Your dispatcher follows them on every call.

Can one person do reception and dispatch?

For many small and mid-sized shops, yes. Answering, booking and dispatching are often handled by the same person. At higher call volume we split the roles.

How does a remote dispatcher talk to my techs?

By phone, text and your field-service app, exactly as an in-office dispatcher would.

Can the dispatcher see where my techs are?

If your software or fleet tracking shows locations, your dispatcher uses it to route jobs.

What size business needs a dispatcher?

Usually once you have two or more techs and the owner is spending part of every day rearranging the schedule.

Can you dispatch subcontractors?

Yes, as long as they can receive job details by phone, text or your software.
